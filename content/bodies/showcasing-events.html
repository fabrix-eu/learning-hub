What is a showcasing event?

\n

A showcasing event is a curated moment where one or more SMEs make their practice visible. Instead of only describing circular fashion, the event allows people to see, touch, hear and discuss real examples such as repaired garments, upcycled products, collect-back services, prototypes, materials, labels, studio processes, digital tools, heritage techniques or local production stories.

\n

Key objective

\n

The main objective is to create visibility, trust and new relationships around local circular production. A showcasing event helps SMEs explain what they do, helps visitors understand the value of circular work and helps facilitators connect makers with buyers, policy actors, mentors, journalists, researchers or other businesses.

\n

Who should participate?

\n

The core participants are SMEs, designers, makers, circular innovators and facilitators. The audience can include retailers, brands, suppliers, public authorities, universities, journalists, students, cultural organisations, clients and citizens. The method works best when the organiser brings together people who would not normally meet, for example small designers and institutional actors, or circular startups and established fashion brands.

\n

What do you need to organise it?

\n

The organiser needs a clear theme, a simple venue plan, a short agenda, a selection of participating SMEs, exhibit labels, photographs or products, communication visuals, registration and GDPR forms, a guided tour or presentation flow, and a networking moment. If the event includes a panel, the questions should connect the showcased examples to the wider challenges of the ecosystem, such as regulation, materials, local production, customer behaviour, traceability, cost and collaboration.

\n

Simple process

\n

The process can be organised in six steps: first, choose the purpose of the showcase and the target audience; second, select SMEs and ask each one to prepare one clear story; third, collect photos, short descriptions and labels; fourth, design the visitor journey, including arrival, tour, talks and networking; fifth, document the event with photos, attendance and notes; sixth, follow up with the SMEs and visitors so that visibility becomes matchmaking, mentoring or collaboration.

\n

How did we use it in FABRIX Athens?

\n

In FABRIX Athens, the showcasing method was used in several forms. During the Greek Fashion Festival and DRAMODE, locally manufactured designers were presented inside a cultural environment that connected fashion, theatre, music and public dialogue. During Seam the Change in Ten Million Hands Impact Hub Athens on the 13th November SMEs pitched their innovations to a Jury of Experts from Academia and Industry Associations, after having hacked their idea and being mentored in the Hackathon. During GR-I-CE 2026, the Meet the Makers session gave awarded SMEs a role inside a circular economy conference, where they presented business models and met policy, research and industry actors. During Fashion Beyond Waste in ESEE INEMY, prototypes created through SME pairings were exhibited together with a policy and market discussion on EPR, the Digital Product Passport, traceability and circular services.

\n

Across these cases, the method worked because it translated abstract concepts such as circular manufacturing, eco-design, textile reuse, reverse logistics, heritage and data into visible examples. It also showed that showcasing is not only communication; it is a practical ecosystem tool, because it can lead to matchmaking meetings, press visibility, new partnerships, policy discussion and wider public recognition of small businesses.

\n

Expected outputs

\n

Typical outputs include an event agenda, exhibit labels, a list of showcased SMEs, photos, short captions, signature lists, GDPR records, press or social media material, visitor feedback, matchmaking notes and a short report. The strongest output is a clearer public narrative about why local circular fashion matters and how SMEs can contribute to the transition.
